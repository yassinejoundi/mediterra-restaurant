# Interview Progress

## Source Context Summary

Brand/Client: Mediterra Restaurant

Industry: Restaurant / Italian dining

Market: Marrakech, Morocco

Website Type: New premium restaurant website

Primary Goal: Drive table reservations through a reservation form, with WhatsApp and phone as secondary CTAs.

Known Direction: Premium yet warm Italian/Mediterranean dining destination. Romantic, elegant, inviting, refined but approachable. Core offer includes handmade pasta, pizza, refined Italian classics, fresh ingredients, and a premium dinner experience.

Known Audience: Primary audience is tourists and couples seeking a premium Italian dining experience in Marrakech. Secondary audience is local residents and expats seeking an elegant but welcoming restaurant for dinners, dates, or social occasions.

Known Constraints: Final colors, address, contact details, opening hours, Google Maps link, real menu, pricing, real reviews, real photography, domain, hosting, form handling, analytics, CMS, and booking/payment integrations are unconfirmed.

## Completed Areas

### Founder Story / Origin Story

Question:
Why does Mediterra truly exist beyond being an Italian restaurant in Marrakech?

Answer:
Mediterra was created by two friends: one Italian living in Marrakech and one Moroccan chef friend who is in love with Italian food and the Italian dining experience. Their personal names should remain hidden.

Strategic Insight:
The brand has a strong cross-cultural origin: Italian lived experience meeting Moroccan culinary passion. This gives Mediterra a more human and differentiated foundation than a generic Italian restaurant. The public story should protect founder anonymity while still expressing the friendship, shared obsession, and cultural bridge behind the restaurant. The strongest positioning angle is not simply "authentic Italian food," but Italian soul interpreted with Marrakech warmth by people who genuinely live between both worlds.

Follow-Up Opportunities:
- Clarify whether the Italian founder influences recipes, hospitality, sourcing, atmosphere, or overall cultural authenticity.
- Clarify the Moroccan chef's culinary background and what specifically made him fall in love with Italian cuisine.
- Define how much of the two-friends story should appear publicly without names.

Confidence:
7/10

### Chef Philosophy / Italian Influence

Question:
What exactly made the Moroccan chef fall in love with Italian food and the Italian dining experience?

Answer:
He discovered Italian cuisine through working with Italian chefs and became obsessed with the discipline behind simple ingredients. He loved how Italian dining turns simple food into a generous, emotional ritual around the table.

Strategic Insight:
This gives Mediterra a distinctive culinary philosophy: simplicity is not treated as casual or basic, but as disciplined, emotional, and generous. The chef's love of Italian cuisine is rooted in craft and ritual rather than trend-chasing. This can become a major positioning pillar: Mediterra celebrates the quiet mastery behind handmade Italian food and the warmth of gathering around the table.

Follow-Up Opportunities:
- Identify specific dishes that best express disciplined simplicity.
- Define service rituals that make meals feel generous and emotional.
- Clarify whether the menu should emphasize handmade craft, ingredient restraint, or family-style generosity.

Confidence:
8/10

### Emotional Experience

Question:
How should guests feel during the Mediterra experience, from arrival to the final moment at the table?

Answer:
Guests should experience all four proposed emotional states: arrive from the energy of Marrakech and feel like they have stepped into a calmer Mediterranean evening; couples should feel taken care of without the service feeling formal or distant; tourists should feel confident they chose somewhere beautiful, reliable, and worth dressing up for; locals should feel it is elegant enough for a special night but relaxed enough to return often.

Strategic Insight:
Mediterra's emotional positioning is a refined Mediterranean refuge inside Marrakech: elegant enough to elevate the evening, warm enough to remove intimidation, and emotionally generous enough to become memorable. The brand should avoid cold luxury. Its premium signal should come from atmosphere, care, craft, and confidence rather than stiffness. The relationship model is best described as a gracious host: attentive, warm, culturally fluent, and quietly refined.

Follow-Up Opportunities:
- Define specific service details that create attentive but informal care.
- Identify visual and sensory cues that create the calmer Mediterranean evening.
- Decide whether romance should be the dominant emotional lead or one of several use cases.

Confidence:
8/10

Question:
Which dishes, rituals, or service details make Mediterra's experience specific instead of generic?

Pasta is made in-house and finished tableside. Guests receive a signature welcome moment when they arrive. Italian recipes are respected, but presented with Marrakech hospitality.

The phrase "Italian recipes respected, presented with Marrakech hospitality" is a strong positioning seed because it balances authenticity with local warmth.

Follow-Up Opportunities:
- Define the exact signature welcome moment.
- Identify the pasta dishes or menu items that should become hero content.
- Clarify what "Marrakech hospitality" means in service behaviors.

### Competitive Frame

Question:
Who does Mediterra compete with or get compared against when tourists or locals choose dinner in Marrakech?

Answer:
Mediterra competes with rooftop restaurants with a view and trendy Instagram restaurants in Gueliz or the Medina.

Strategic Insight:
Mediterra is not only competing in the Italian food category. It competes in the premium-night-out category, where guests often choose based on atmosphere, social proof, perceived beauty, and memorability. Against rooftop and Instagram-led restaurants, Mediterra should avoid trying to out-spectacle them. Its stronger position is a more intimate, craft-led, emotionally warm alternative: a place where the memory comes from the table, the pasta, the hospitality, and the romance of the evening rather than just the view or photo moment.

Follow-Up Opportunities:
- Identify what Mediterra offers that view-led restaurants cannot.
- Define the right balance between Instagrammable visuals and authentic dining substance.
- Clarify whether Mediterra wants to feel quieter and more intimate or equally buzz-worthy.

Confidence:
8/10

Why should someone choose Mediterra instead of a rooftop or trendy Instagram restaurant?

Guests choose Mediterra when they want beauty with substance: handmade food, attentive hospitality, and a romantic room that does not feel performative. Mediterra is for people who want the evening to feel personal, not staged.

This should influence messaging, homepage hero copy, About page narrative, and photography direction.

Follow-Up Opportunities:

- Define how the dining room visually communicates romance without feeling staged.
- Identify proof assets for handmade food and attentive hospitality.

### Visual Strategy

Answer:
Coastal Italy with a Marrakech riad warmth touch.

The visual system should not become generic Italian rustic or generic Moroccan decor. The lead should be coastal Italian ease: warm ivory, natural textures, olive, soft terracotta, linen, fresh ingredients, relaxed elegance. This creates a hybrid visual identity that feels appropriate to Marrakech while protecting the Italian heart of the restaurant.

Follow-Up Opportunities:
- Define exact color palette and material cues.

- Build a future photography shot list around coastal light, handmade pasta, candlelit tables, and riad warmth.

Follow-Up Opportunities:
- Build reservation CTAs into the hero, sticky mobile navigation, menu page, and gallery sections.

What does the name "Mediterra" mean to you, or what should people feel it means when they hear it?

"Mediterra" means a Mediterranean table grounded in warmth, earth, and generosity. It evokes coastal Italian ease with the warmth and texture of Marrakech.

### Brand Archetype

Question:
Should the final brand feel more like a Lover archetype, focused on romance, sensory pleasure, and intimacy, or a Caregiver/Host archetype, focused on warmth, attention, and generosity?

Answer:
A mix.

Strategic Insight:
Mediterra should blend the Lover and Caregiver/Host archetypes. The Lover should lead the external emotional appeal through romance, sensory pleasure, candlelit atmosphere, beauty, intimacy, and desire. The Caregiver/Host should shape the service behavior through warmth, personal attention, generosity, and welcome. This mix protects the brand from becoming either overly sensual and theatrical or overly service-oriented and plain.

Follow-Up Opportunities:
- Use Lover-led language for homepage and visual direction.
- Use Caregiver/Host cues for reservation, service promise, and trust-building copy.
- Keep the Creator archetype as a tertiary influence through handmade pasta and tableside craft, but not the dominant psychology.

Confidence:
8/10

### Signature Welcome Moment

Question:
What should the signature welcome moment be, or should the brand persona leave it as a future operational detail?

Answer:
A small olive oil, bread, or focaccia moment tied to Italian simplicity, plus a discreet personal check-in that makes guests feel expected, not processed.

Strategic Insight:
This arrival ritual perfectly expresses Mediterra's brand philosophy: simple ingredients, Italian discipline, Marrakech generosity, and personal attention. It creates a tangible beginning to the guest journey without feeling gimmicky. The language "expected, not processed" is strategically useful because it reinforces the anti-rushed, anti-performative positioning.

Follow-Up Opportunities:
- Decide whether the welcome bread/focaccia is complimentary or simply presented as part of the experience.
- Photograph this ritual for website storytelling.
- Train staff to make the check-in warm but discreet.

### Brand Guardrails / What To Avoid

Question:
Are there any words, styles, or claims Mediterra should avoid because they would make the brand feel wrong?

Answer:
All proposed examples should be avoided: "fine dining" if it implies stiffness, "Instagrammable," cliché phrases like "a taste of Italy" or "culinary journey," loud luxury language like "exclusive," "opulent," or "VIP," visuals that feel nightclub-like, overly Moroccan-themed, or generic rustic Italian, and unsupported claims like "the best Italian restaurant."

Strategic Insight:
The brand must protect its restraint. Mediterra should sound premium without luxury clichés, romantic without melodrama, Italian without generic trattoria tropes, and visually beautiful without chasing Instagram culture. Its credibility depends on specificity and proof rather than exaggerated claims.

Follow-Up Opportunities:
- Use concrete sensory language instead of generic restaurant claims.
- Keep website copy grounded in handmade pasta, tableside finishing, warm arrival rituals, and real atmosphere.
- Avoid over-staging photography; prioritize natural, intimate, believable scenes.

Confidence:
9/10
