# Design System: Mediterra

## 1. Visual Theme & Atmosphere

Mediterra's visual world should feel like a romantic Mediterranean table in Marrakech: intimate, warm, editorial, tactile, and quietly premium. The site should not feel like a restaurant chasing spectacle. It should feel like an evening slowing down: candlelight on linen, handmade pasta being finished at the table, olive oil catching a soft glow, warm plaster walls, dark wood, and guests who feel expected rather than processed.

The atmosphere is led by the Lover archetype, expressed through romance, sensory detail, softness, and a sense of personal occasion. The Caregiver/Host archetype gives the system its warmth, generosity, and reassuring hospitality. The Creator appears through craft cues: handmade textures, close details, considered composition, and visible evidence of discipline behind simple ingredients.

The aesthetic philosophy is beauty with substance. Every visual decision should make the restaurant feel beautiful, but never hollow. The site should look premium because it is composed, restrained, tactile, and emotionally specific, not because it uses loud luxury signals. The visual language should sit between coastal Italian ease and Marrakech riad warmth: lighter, calmer, and more Mediterranean than an ornate Moroccan restaurant; warmer and more human than a cold luxury dining brand.

The mood should be sun-warmed by day and candlelit by night. Density should be moderate and breathable, with generous pauses around important imagery and copy. The character should be romantic but not dramatic, elegant but never cold, sensory but not excessive, and refined without becoming stiff.

Mediterra should never feel nightclub-like, over-Moroccanized, generic rustic Italian, tourist-brochure polished, corporate, sterile, or designed around a single social media photo moment. It should avoid heavy ornament, glossy black-and-gold luxury, clichéd Italian trattoria tropes, exaggerated Tuscan rusticity, and anything that feels performative rather than personal.

Visitors should feel reassured, drawn in, and quietly confident that this is a place worthy of one of their limited evenings in Marrakech. The emotional response should be: this looks beautiful, but it also feels real.

## 2. Color Palette & Roles

### Warm Ivory Plaster

Hex Code: `#F7F0E4`

Purpose: The primary environmental foundation for the website.

Usage: Use across main page backgrounds, spacious editorial sections, reservation areas, story content, and any surface that should feel calm, sun-warmed, and welcoming.

Psychological Impact: Communicates warmth, ease, and hospitality without feeling casual. It gives the site the feeling of a Mediterranean room with plaster walls and soft evening light.

---

### Natural Linen Cream

Hex Code: `#EFE2CF`

Purpose: A tactile secondary surface that creates gentle separation.

Usage: Use for menu panels, reservation form surfaces, quote areas, soft content containers, table-card-like sections, and background blocks that should feel intimate without becoming boxed-in.

Psychological Impact: Suggests linen, bread, handmade paper, and table warmth. It supports the Caregiver/Host personality by making information feel approachable and considered.

---

### Olive Grove Green

Hex Code: `#6F7651`

Purpose: The primary Mediterranean accent.

Usage: Use for secondary actions, small labels, active navigation cues, section markers, category accents, proof-point details, and moments connected to ingredients, freshness, or hospitality.

Psychological Impact: Signals Mediterranean freshness, olive oil, calm confidence, and natural grounding. It keeps the palette from becoming overly romantic or heavy.

---

### Soft Terracotta Clay

Hex Code: `#B86F4B`

Purpose: The earth-warmth accent that brings Marrakech texture into the system.

Usage: Use sparingly for small dividers, dish-price details, image captions, quote marks, story accents, and warm transition moments between sections.

Psychological Impact: Adds human warmth, craft, and materiality. It should feel like clay, sun, and handmade ceramic rather than bright orange decoration.

---

### Deep Candlelit Wine

Hex Code: `#5A1F2B`

Purpose: The romantic depth color and primary conversion color.

Usage: Use for the main reservation action, high-intent CTA areas, selected emphasis, footer depth, and intimate evening sections where the brand needs more drama and focus.

Psychological Impact: Creates romance, confidence, appetite, and quiet occasion. It supports the Lover archetype without becoming theatrical or flashy.

---

### Espresso Charcoal

Hex Code: `#2A211D`

Purpose: The primary text and contrast color.

Usage: Use for headlines, body copy, navigation, footer content, dark surfaces, and strong editorial moments that require clarity and weight.

Psychological Impact: Feels grounded, mature, and restaurant-like. It is warmer than black, giving contrast without coldness.

---

### Muted Brass Glow

Hex Code: `#C29A5B`

Purpose: A discreet premium highlight.

Usage: Use only in restrained details: review stars, fine rules, small decorative separators, candlelight accents, or special hospitality notes. It should never become a dominant gold theme.

Psychological Impact: Adds subtle luminosity and evening warmth. It gives the site a premium finish while avoiding loud luxury language.

---

### Soft Sage Mist

Hex Code: `#D8D7C2`

Purpose: A quiet atmospheric support tint.

Usage: Use for gentle badges, background washes, quiet proof-point areas, gallery filters, and transitions where olive needs to become softer and more breathable.

Psychological Impact: Creates calm, freshness, and softness. It balances the deeper wine and espresso tones with a more relaxed Mediterranean feeling.

---

### Candle Smoke Taupe

Hex Code: `#8A7A69`

Purpose: A subdued utility tone for secondary text and understated information.

Usage: Use for captions, metadata, menu descriptions, helper text, address details, form hints, and secondary navigation.

Psychological Impact: Adds refinement and quietness. It lets practical information exist without competing with the emotional story.

## 3. Typography Rules

Typography should feel premium, editorial, romantic, and highly readable. It should carry the elegance of a restaurant editorial without becoming decorative, old-fashioned, or theatrical.

Recommended headline family: `Playfair Display`, `Cormorant Garamond`, or a similarly graceful high-contrast editorial serif. The selected headline face should feel romantic and composed, suitable for a wordmark and large atmospheric statements. It should have enough character to make Mediterra memorable without relying on an icon.

Recommended body family: `Inter`, `Manrope`, or `Source Sans 3`. The body face should be clean, modern, and calm, allowing menu details, reservation instructions, FAQ content, and location information to be read quickly by tourists on mobile.

The Mediterra wordmark should remain text-only. It should use a refined serif with elegant contrast and soft restraint. Avoid scripts, exaggerated swashes, generic luxury capitals, rustic Tuscan lettering, or anything that feels like nightlife branding.

Hierarchy should be built through scale, rhythm, and whitespace rather than aggressive weight. Large headlines should feel cinematic and intimate, with relaxed line breaks. Section headings should feel editorial and composed. Body copy should use generous line spacing so the reading experience feels calm, like a host speaking with poise rather than a restaurant shouting offers.

Weight usage should be restrained. Headlines should use regular or medium weights whenever possible. Body copy should remain regular. Labels, navigation, and form fields may use medium weight for clarity, but should not feel heavy. Bold should be reserved for meaningful emphasis, not decoration.

Letter spacing should be subtle. Small labels and navigation can use slight tracking to feel premium and deliberate. Large romantic headlines should avoid excessive spacing because the brand is intimate, not corporate or fashion-cold.

The reading experience should follow the brand's voice: poetic first, then grounded proof. A headline may create atmosphere, but supporting copy should immediately clarify the tangible promise: handmade pasta, warm hospitality, Marrakech location, reservation request, or menu confidence.

## 4. Component Stylings

### Buttons

Primary buttons should feel like a warm invitation to the table, not a loud sales device. The main reservation button should use Deep Candlelit Wine with Warm Ivory Plaster text. Its shape should be softly pill-like, generous in width, and comfortable to touch. It should feel decisive, romantic, and premium.

Secondary buttons should be quieter: transparent or linen-toned surfaces, fine borders, Espresso Charcoal text, and subtle Olive Grove or Terracotta warmth when active. They should support exploration actions such as View Menu, View Gallery, Get Directions, or WhatsApp Us without competing with reservation.

Button language should be concise and hospitable. Visual treatment should avoid sharp corners, harsh shadows, bright colors, or aggressive hover effects. Motion, if used later, should feel like a soft reveal rather than a bounce or flash.

### Cards & Containers

Cards should feel like printed menus, linen-backed notes, or refined table cards rather than generic digital tiles. Surfaces should use Warm Ivory Plaster or Natural Linen Cream, with fine low-contrast edges and softly rounded corners. Shadows should be very gentle, closer to natural candlelit depth than digital elevation.

Menu cards should be structured and calm, giving dish names, descriptions, and prices enough breathing room. Review cards should feel intimate and credible, not promotional. Story containers should feel editorial, with a sense of pause and material warmth.

Avoid dense grids of identical boxes. Use varied proportions, atmospheric image pairings, and editorial pacing to preserve the feeling of a real restaurant rather than a template.

### Navigation

Navigation should feel composed, easy, and reservation-led. The wordmark should be prominent but not oversized. Links should be minimal and calm: Home, Menu, Story, Gallery, Contact, and Reserve a Table.

The reservation action should be visually emphasized in the header as the primary next step. It should always feel available, especially on mobile, because the site exists to convert interest into reservation requests.

Navigation should not feel busy, overly animated, or visually heavy. The experience should be like arriving at a well-hosted restaurant: clear, gracious, and unhurried.

### Forms

Reservation forms should feel reassuring and personal. Fields should be spacious, warm-toned, and easy to complete on mobile. Labels should be clear and calm. Helper text should explain that the reservation is a request and will be confirmed manually by WhatsApp.

Input surfaces should use Warm Ivory Plaster or Natural Linen Cream with fine espresso-tinted borders. Active states should use Olive Grove Green or Deep Candlelit Wine. Avoid cold default colors, harsh outlines, cramped spacing, or overly technical error messages.

The form should feel like sharing details with a host, not submitting to a system. The surrounding copy should reduce uncertainty and make tourists confident that their request will be handled personally.

### CTAs

CTA sections should feel like closing invitations, not pressure blocks. They should combine atmosphere, practical reassurance, and one clear action. A strong CTA might pair a candlelit table image with concise copy about requesting a table and receiving manual WhatsApp confirmation.

Primary CTA areas can use Deep Candlelit Wine or Espresso Charcoal for depth, softened with Warm Ivory text and muted brass details. Lighter CTA areas can use Natural Linen Cream with wine-colored buttons. CTAs should repeat after major trust-building moments: menu preview, gallery proof, story substance, reviews, and location clarity.

### Content Sections

Content sections should be sequenced like an evening unfolding. The first impression should create desire through atmosphere. The next sections should prove food credibility, then hospitality, then trust, then logistical clarity.

Sections should avoid feeling like disconnected blocks. Use repeating visual cues: warm plaster foundations, linen panels, olive and terracotta details, editorial type, atmospheric photography, fine dividers, and generous whitespace. The whole site should feel like one coherent restaurant world.

## 5. Layout Principles

The layout philosophy is editorial hospitality. Pages should feel composed like a premium restaurant feature, but practical enough to help tourists make a confident reservation quickly.

The grid should be disciplined but not rigid. Use a broad central content frame with occasional asymmetric image-and-copy pairings. Large atmospheric images should be allowed to breathe. Text columns should remain comfortable and narrow enough for easy reading. Menu and reservation content should be structured clearly, but softened through spacing and material color.

Whitespace should feel generous and intentional. It represents the brand's restraint: romance without drama, luxury without coldness, beauty without performance. Avoid cramped content, overly dense cards, and excessive competing CTAs.

Visual hierarchy should follow the visitor's decision path. The first screen must immediately communicate Italian dining, Marrakech, romance, handmade craft, and reservation access. After that, hierarchy should move from emotional desire to concrete proof: menu, gallery, story, reviews, location, and reservation.

Content density should be medium-low on brand and atmosphere sections, medium on menu sections, and tightly clear on reservation and FAQ sections. Visitors should never have to work hard to find the next step.

Responsive behavior should prioritize trust and action on mobile. Mobile pages should show atmosphere quickly, keep Reserve a Table prominent, make menu scanning easy, and surface WhatsApp/contact reassurance near decision points. Imagery should remain immersive but not slow or ornamental; it must support confidence.

Layout rhythm should alternate between immersive visual moments and quiet informational panels. This rhythm mirrors the restaurant promise: beauty first, then substance.

## 6. Photography Direction

Photography is a trust asset, not decoration. Images must prove that the restaurant feels as warm and credible in person as it appears online.

The style should be warm editorial documentary. It should feel observed rather than staged, premium rather than glossy, intimate rather than performative. The camera should get close enough to show texture: flour on hands, pasta folds, olive oil, bread crust, candlelight, linen, ceramic, dark wood, plaster, and hands serving dishes.

Lighting should be natural, golden, and candlelit. Daytime images can feel sun-warmed and Mediterranean; evening images should glow softly without becoming dark or nightclub-like. Avoid harsh flash, over-saturated food styling, sterile studio lighting, or exaggerated contrast.

Composition should favor asymmetry, table-level intimacy, and believable human presence. Use close details for craft, wider compositions for room atmosphere, and soft human scenes for hospitality: a couple leaning into conversation, friends sharing dishes, a server finishing pasta at the table, the welcome bread or focaccia moment, and guests arriving into a warm room.

Subject focus should include handmade pasta, pizza, refined Italian classics, tableside finishing, olive oil, bread or focaccia, candlelit tables, intimate interiors, Mediterranean materials, guest conversation, and subtle service gestures. Marrakech should appear through texture, light, and hospitality rather than overt decorative motifs.

The mood should be romantic, generous, tactile, and real. Avoid over-staged influencer imagery, empty luxury interiors, generic stock food, overly rustic trattoria scenes, exaggerated Moroccan styling, or images that promise spectacle instead of substance.

Generated placeholder imagery may be used for early design, but final launch imagery should be replaced with real professional photography as soon as possible. Real photography will be essential for conversion because Mediterra's audience needs visual trust before reserving.

## 7. Design System Notes for Generation

The visual atmosphere should be described as: warm premium Mediterranean restaurant, romantic editorial hospitality, coastal Italian ease with Marrakech riad warmth, candlelit but not dramatic, beautiful with substance, intimate but not exclusive.

Shapes should be soft, tactile, and composed. Use pill-like primary buttons, gently rounded content containers, refined image corners, and soft panel transitions. Avoid sharp corporate geometry, overly playful curves, or heavy decorative frames.

Shadows should be whisper-soft and natural, like depth created by candlelight or warm afternoon light. Avoid strong digital shadows, floating cards, or anything that makes the design feel like a generic SaaS interface.

Color references should remain consistent: Warm Ivory Plaster for the world, Natural Linen Cream for tactile surfaces, Deep Candlelit Wine for reservation intent and romance, Olive Grove Green for Mediterranean freshness, Soft Terracotta Clay for material warmth, Espresso Charcoal for grounded contrast, Muted Brass Glow for tiny premium accents, Soft Sage Mist for quiet atmospheric support, and Candle Smoke Taupe for secondary text.

Typography references should combine a graceful editorial serif for headlines and wordmark with a clean modern sans serif for body, menu, reservations, and practical information. Headlines should feel romantic and composed. Body copy should feel clear, reassuring, and hospitable.

Layout references should include generous editorial spacing, large atmospheric imagery, disciplined but warm grids, asymmetric image-and-copy pairings, calm reservation paths, and mobile-first clarity for tourists deciding quickly.

Component references should include deep wine reservation buttons, linen menu cards, warm form fields, understated review treatments, fine dividers, atmospheric image crops, calm navigation, and CTA sections that feel like personal invitations.

Maintain consistency by checking every design decision against the brand promise: handmade Italian food, attentive hospitality, Mediterranean romance, and an evening that feels personal rather than staged. If a visual choice feels loud, trend-led, cold, overly ornate, generic, or purely decorative, it does not belong in Mediterra's world.
